## 1. Result

**Mathematical finding: the Perron–Frobenius argument is valid under the stated finite-dimensional hypotheses when applied to (K=cI-H), not to (H+cI).**

For finite (N\ge1), real (J_{ij}), and a strictly positive uniform transverse field (h>0), the Hamiltonian is a real symmetric matrix in the computational basis. Its ground-state eigenvalue is simple, and its normalized ground-state vector can be chosen strictly positive in that basis.

This validates the mathematical conclusion through an independently reconstructed argument. It does **not** validate the incorrect shift sentence in the Paper A manuscript, nor complete formal verification or the full proof-to-manuscript audit. The Constant Llapoy claim remains **NOT_VERIFIED** pending those separate obligations.

## 2. Independent derivation

Let computational basis vectors be indexed by bit strings (s\in\{0,1\}^N). The Ising part
[
-sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\sigma_i^z\sigma_j^z
]
is diagonal, with real diagonal entries because every (J_{ij}\in\mathbb R).

The transverse-field term
[
-h\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma_i^x
]
has matrix entry (-h) exactly between configurations differing in one bit, and zero for other off-diagonal pairs. Since (h>0), these entries are strictly negative.

Choose a real scalar (c\ge\max_s H_{ss}). Define
[
K=cI-H.
]
Then:
1. Every diagonal entry satisfies (K_{ss}=c-H_{ss}\ge0).
2. Every single-bit-flip off-diagonal entry is (K_{st}=+h>0).
3. Every other off-diagonal entry is zero.

The undirected graph on bit strings with edges for single-bit flips is the (N)-dimensional hypercube. It is connected for every finite (N\ge1): any bit string can be transformed into any other by flipping the finitely many differing coordinates. Therefore the nonnegative matrix (K) is irreducible.

The Perron–Frobenius theorem for an irreducible nonnegative finite matrix gives a positive spectral radius (r(K)), algebraically simple, with a strictly positive eigenvector. Because (K) is real symmetric, all its eigenvalues are real and its spectral radius is its largest algebraic eigenvalue. The eigenvalues satisfy
[
\lambda_j(K)=c-E_j(H).
]
Consequently the largest eigenvalue of (K) corresponds exactly to the smallest eigenvalue of (H). Simplicity of the former gives simplicity of the ground-state eigenvalue of (H); the corresponding eigenvector is strictly positive in the computational basis.

No sign or magnitude restriction on the real couplings (J_{ij}) is needed for this argument. Their values affect only the diagonal entries, which are absorbed by the choice of (c).

## 3. Hypothesis and boundary audit

- **Finite (N\ge1):** required for a finite matrix; (N=1) is covered (the two-vertex hypercube is connected).
- **Uniform (h>0):** essential to make every hypercube edge strictly positive in (K). At (h=0), degeneracy can occur, so the theorem cannot be extended to that boundary by this argument.
- **Real (J_{ij}):** ensures a real diagonal contribution and a real symmetric Hamiltonian. No ferromagnetic condition (J_{ij}\ge0) is required.
- **Connectedness:** more generally, the exact requirement is connectedness of the configuration graph generated by strictly positive spin-flip matrix elements. Merely saying that a physical-site graph is connected is not enough unless it implies configuration-space irreducibility.
- **Choice of (c):** exists because the matrix is finite-dimensional; it need only dominate the finitely many diagonal entries of (H).

## 4. Source defect and repair boundary

The Paper A manuscript states that adding a sufficiently large multiple of the identity to (H) makes the matrix nonnegative. That statement is false: adding (cI) changes only diagonal entries and leaves the (-h) off-diagonal entries negative.

The correct matrix is (cI-H) (equivalently (-H+cI)). The auxiliary proof source uses this correct sign. This is a repair to the proof argument, not a silent correction to the frozen manuscript source.

## 5. Analyticity consequence — limited scope

For a finite-dimensional real-analytic matrix family (H(x)), a simple isolated eigenvalue has a locally real-analytic spectral projection (for example, by a resolvent contour integral around that eigenvalue). The dimensionless family in the auxiliary source has a simple ground state for every finite (x\ge0), since its transverse field is (h=1).

This conclusion concerns the ground-state spectral projector. It does not, by itself, prove strict positivity of reduced density matrices, entropy analyticity at all required points, or any nonvanishing second-derivative/curvature assertion. Those require separate obligations.
