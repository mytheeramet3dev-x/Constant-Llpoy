# Section 1: Introduction

## 1.1 Foundational review of self-consistent entanglement networks

In the companion paper (Paper 1), we formulated the bifurcation problem for autonomous quantum networks whose interaction couplings are generated by correlations of the ground state they produce. The model is an all-to-all transverse-field Ising system on the complete graph \(K_N\):

\[
H[\mathbf J]
=-\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\sigma_i^z\sigma_j^z
-h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_i^x,
\]

with the self-consistency condition

\[
J_{ij}=g\,I_{ij}[\Psi_0(\mathbf J)],
\]

where \(g>0\) is the feedback strength and \(I_{ij}\) is the bipartite mutual information of the ground state.

For the permutation-symmetric configuration \(J_{ij}=J\), the positive energy-rescaling homogeneity of the Hamiltonian gives the dimensionless variable \(x=J/h\) and the dimensionless mutual-information function

\[
I_{12}(J,h)=\mathcal G_N(x).
\]

The corresponding dimensionless fixed-point equation is

\[
f(x;\eta)=\eta\mathcal G_N(x)-x=0,
\qquad \eta=\frac gh.
\]

Under the appropriate interior-attainment and nondegeneracy assumptions, Paper 1 established the following foundations:

1. **Threshold scaling.** The threshold has the dimensional form
\[
g_c(h)=\alpha_Nh,
\qquad
\alpha_N=\inf_{x>0}\frac{x}{\mathcal G_N(x)}.
\]
Here \(\alpha_N\) is a system-size-dependent scaling coefficient, not an \(N\)-independent universal constant.

2. **Conditional local saddle-node structure.** An interior nondegenerate tangent point gives two nearby local branches on the supercritical side. This is a local theorem; Paper 1 does not claim a globally complete root count for every parameter value.

3. **Scale-independent dimensionless derivative.** Along \(g=\gamma\alpha_Nh\), the dimensionless fixed-point derivative is
\[
f_x(x_*;\eta)
=\gamma\alpha_N\mathcal G_N'(x_*)-1.
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
F(J;g,h)=gI_{12}(J,h)-J,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\partial F}{\partial J}\bigg|_{J_*}
=\gamma\alpha_N\mathcal G_N'(x_*)-1
}.
\]
The corresponding Picard multiplier is
\[
T'(x_*)=\gamma\alpha_N\mathcal G_N'(x_*),
\]
and scalar Picard attractivity requires \(|T'(x_*)|<1\).

These results identify the symmetric branch on which the symmetry-breaking response problem should be evaluated. They do not, by themselves, establish emergent geometry or full edge-space stability.

## 1.2 The emergent geometry question

The existence of a self-consistent permutation-symmetric network does not determine whether spatial locality or finite-dimensional geometric structure can emerge. In an ab initio approach, such structure would have to arise from symmetry-breaking deformations of the initially unstructured interaction topology.

At the symmetric point, infinitesimal edge perturbations belong to the edge representation

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{edge}}
=\mathcal V_0\oplus\mathcal V_1\oplus\mathcal V_2,
\]

with

\[
\dim\mathcal V_0=1,\qquad
\dim\mathcal V_1=N-1,\qquad
\dim\mathcal V_2=\frac{N(N-3)}2.
\]

\(\mathcal V_0\) is the uniform sector, \(\mathcal V_1\) is the degree-heterogeneous sector, and \(\mathcal V_2\) is the degree-preserving residual sector. The latter is a natural representation sector in which distributed graph deformations may occur, but representation labels alone do not prove the emergence of a lattice, translation invariance, or finite spatial dimension.

Assuming the response Jacobian exists and is \(S_N\)-equivariant, it is determined by

\[
A=\frac{\partial I_{12}}{\partial J_{12}},\qquad
B=\frac{\partial I_{12}}{\partial J_{13}},\qquad
C=\frac{\partial I_{12}}{\partial J_{34}}.
\]

The nontrivial sector eigenvalues obey the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\chi_1-\chi_2=(N-2)(B-C)}.
\]

Thus \(B-C>0\) is equivalent to \(\chi_1>\chi_2\). This is an exact response-ordering identity, not yet an exact dynamical instability theorem.

## 1.3 Scope delimitation: the linear response regime

This paper studies the linear response of the permutation-symmetric fixed point within \(\mathcal V_{\mathrm{edge}}\).

1. We evaluate the response Jacobian and its \(S_N\) sectors, together with the initial linear growth or decay rates of symmetry-breaking perturbations.
2. We do not analyze higher-order mode coupling, nonlinear saturation, secondary branches, limit cycles, or other nonequilibrium nonlinear phenomena.
3. We formulate the main conclusion as a conditional obstruction: if \(B-C>0\), then the degree-heterogeneous response sector has the larger response eigenvalue than the degree-preserving sector, \(\chi_1>\chi_2\).

The sign \(B-C>0\) is currently a strong numerically supported conjecture for the reliable tested cases, not a closed-form algebraic theorem. The present paper therefore separates the exact sector identity from the unresolved sign claim.

## 1.4 Organization of the paper

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows:

* **Section 2** formulates the edge-space perturbation theory and defines the response Jacobian.
* **Section 3** derives the \(S_N\) representation decomposition and the associated projection operators.

# Section 2: Edge-Space Perturbation Theory

## 2.1 Symmetric fixed point

We evaluate symmetry-breaking response at a permutation-symmetric self-consistent point
\[
J_{ij}=J_*,
\qquad
i<j,
\]
on the selected branch discussed in Paper 1. The perturbation is an edge-weight variation
\[
J_{ij}=J_*+\delta J_{ij}.
\]
The vector of independent edge perturbations belongs to
\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{edge}}\cong\mathbb R^{\binom N2}.
\]

The ground-state mutual-information map is
\[
\mathbf J\longmapsto \mathbf I(\mathbf J),
\qquad
\mathbf I=\{I_{ij}\}_{i<j}.
\]
Assuming differentiability at the symmetric point, its linearization is
\[
\delta I_{ij}
=
\sum_{k<l}
\chi_{(ij),(kl)}\,\delta J_{kl}
+
o(\|\delta\mathbf J\|),
\]
where
\[
\chi_{(ij),(kl)}
=
\left.
\frac{\partial I_{ij}}{\partial J_{kl}}
\right|_{\mathbf J=J_*\mathbf 1}.
\]

**[EXACT, conditional on differentiability]** The linearized self-consistency map is
\[
\delta\mathbf J'
=
g\chi\,\delta\mathbf J.
\]
Thus \(g\chi\) gives the linearized feedback eigenvalues. A complete nonlinear stability statement is outside the scope of this paper.

## 2.2 Equivariance

For every permutation \(\pi\in S_N\), let \(U_\pi\) denote the induced action on edge space. The Hamiltonian and mutual-information map are permutation-equivariant:
\[
\mathbf I(U_\pi\mathbf J)
=
U_\pi\mathbf I(\mathbf J).
\]
Differentiating at the invariant symmetric point gives
\[
\chi U_\pi=U_\pi\chi.
\]

**[EXACT, conditional on differentiability]** The response operator belongs to the commutant of the edge representation of \(S_N\). Consequently, it preserves the isotypic sectors described in Section 3.

## 2.3 Response ordering versus dynamical stability

The quantities \(\chi_k\) are response eigenvalues. The feedback eigenvalues are \(g\chi_k\). Therefore:
- \(\chi_1>\chi_2\) is a response-ordering statement;
- instability requires comparison with the chosen dynamical criterion and the relevant reference multiplier;
- \(\chi_1>\chi_2\) alone does not prove that \(\mathcal V_1\) is unstable or that \(\mathcal V_2\) is stable.

This distinction is essential because the symmetric branch has a separate uniform-sector condition, while the present paper studies nonuniform edge perturbations.

# Section 3: \(S_N\) Decomposition and the Johnson Scheme

## 3.1 Edge representation

Identify an edge perturbation with a symmetric zero-diagonal matrix \(X=(X_{ij})\). The edge space is the permutation representation on two-element subsets of \(\{1,\dots,N\}\). For \(N\ge4\), it decomposes as
\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{edge}}
=
\mathcal V_0\oplus\mathcal V_1\oplus\mathcal V_2,
\]
with
\[
\dim\mathcal V_0=1,\qquad
\dim\mathcal V_1=N-1,\qquad
\dim\mathcal V_2=\frac{N(N-3)}2.
\]
The dimensions sum to
\[
1+(N-1)+\frac{N(N-3)}2=\binom N2.
\]

The three sectors correspond respectively to the irreducible components associated with the Young types \([N]\), \([N-1,1]\), and \([N-2,2]\).

**[EXACT]** This is the standard decomposition of the two-subset permutation representation, equivalently the eigenspace decomposition of the Johnson association scheme \(J(N,2)\) (Brouwer, Cohen, and Neumaier, 1989).

## 3.2 Explicit sector descriptions

The trivial sector is
\[
\mathcal V_0=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_E\},
\]
where every edge has equal weight.

For a vertex vector \(u\in\mathbb R^N\) with \(\sum_i u_i=0\), define
\[
(X_u)_{ij}=u_i+u_j.
\]
The span of these matrices is \(\mathcal V_1\), and has dimension \(N-1\). These perturbations change vertex degrees and are therefore degree-heterogeneous.

The residual sector is
\[
\mathcal V_2
=
\left\{
X=X^\mathsf T:
X_{ii}=0,\ 
\sum_{j\ne i}X_{ij}=0\ \text{for every }i
\right\}.
\]
It has dimension \(N(N-3)/2\) and preserves all vertex degrees to first order.

**[EXACT]** The direct-sum decomposition and the dimension formulas follow by separating the uniform component, the degree vector, and the degree-zero residual.

## 3.3 Johnson-scheme commutant

At the symmetric point, two edge pairs are classified by their intersection size:
- identical edges;
- distinct edges sharing one vertex;
- disjoint edges.

Therefore every \(S_N\)-equivariant operator on edge space has the form
\[
\chi=A I+B A_1+C A_2,
\]
where \(A_1\) is the adjacency matrix for shared-node edge pairs and \(A_2\) is the adjacency matrix for disjoint edge pairs.

The three operators \(I,A_1,A_2\) span the Bose–Mesner algebra of \(J(N,2)\). Hence the response operator is scalar on each irreducible sector.

**[EXACT, conditional on differentiability and equivariance]** The Johnson-scheme structure reduces the full response matrix to the three local derivatives \(A,B,C\).

# Section 4: Sector Eigenvalues and Exact Identity

## 4.1 Local derivative parameters

Fix the observed edge \((1,2)\). Define
\[
A=
\frac{\partial I_{12}}{\partial J_{12}},
\qquad
B=
\frac{\partial I_{12}}{\partial J_{13}},
\qquad
C=
\frac{\partial I_{12}}{\partial J_{34}},
\]
where \((1,3)\) is a shared-node edge and \((3,4)\) is a disjoint edge.

By permutation symmetry, these values do not depend on the particular labels, provided the intersection type is unchanged.

## 4.2 Sector eigenvalues

The adjacency counts in the Johnson scheme give:
- each edge has \(2(N-2)\) shared-node neighbors;
- each edge has \(\binom{N-2}{2}\) disjoint neighbors.

The response eigenvalue on the uniform sector is
\[
\chi_0=A+2(N-2)B+\binom{N-2}{2}C.
\]
The two nontrivial sector eigenvalues are
\[
\chi_1=A+(N-4)B-(N-3)C,
\]
and
\[
\chi_2=A-2B+C.
\]

**[EXACT]** These formulas follow by applying \(I,A_1,A_2\) to the three Johnson-scheme eigenspaces.

**[VALIDATED NUMERICAL]** Their implementation agrees with the dense-versus-
reduced response cross-checks at the documented validated checkpoints.

At a general symmetric point $x_*=J_*/h$, the uniform-sector response is
\[
\chi_0(x_*)=\frac{\mathcal G_N'(x_*)}{h}.
\]
At the tangent point this reduces to \(\chi_0(x_c)=1/g_c\). The response
notation here is distinct from the scalar fixed-point derivative in Paper 1.

## 4.3 Exact sector-gap identity

Subtracting the two nontrivial eigenvalues:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi_1-\chi_2
&=
\bigl[A+(N-4)B-(N-3)C\bigr]
-\bigl[A-2B+C\bigr]\\
&=(N-2)B-(N-2)C\\
&=\boxed{(N-2)(B-C)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

**[EXACT]** Therefore
\[
B-C>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi_1>\chi_2
\]
for \(N>2\).

This identity is algebraic and does not rely on numerical diagonalization, perturbative truncation, or a physical interpretation of the sectors.

**[VALIDATED NUMERICAL]** The implementation of this identity and the
reduced-versus-dense response values agree within the documented numerical
cross-check tolerance at the validated checkpoints. This numerical check does
not promote the sign of (B-C) to a theorem.

# Section 5: Conditional Obstruction

## 5.1 Conditional statement

The exact identity converts the sign question for local response derivatives into a sector-ordering question:
\[
B-C>0
\Longleftrightarrow
\chi_1>\chi_2.
\]

**[CONJECTURE]** For the all-to-all TFIM on the selected symmetric branch, the sign \(B-C>0\) holds throughout the reliable tested regime.

The corresponding obstruction is conditional:

> If \(B-C>0\), and if the relevant feedback dynamics amplify sectors according to the ordering of \(g\chi_k\), then degree-heterogeneous perturbations have larger linear response than degree-preserving perturbations.

This is not an unconditional impossibility theorem for emergent geometry. It does not exclude nonlinear stabilization, different feedback observables, extra constraints, or a model in which the sign reverses.

The resulting preference is, at linear order and under hypothesis $B-C>0$,
for hub-like degree heterogeneity over the degree-preserving tensor sector.

## 5.2 What the sign does and does not prove

If \(B-C>0\), then
\[
\chi_1>\chi_2.
\]
This establishes relative response ordering. To infer instability one must specify:
1. the feedback map;
2. the reference fixed point;
3. whether the relevant multiplier is \(g\chi_k\), \(1+g\chi_k\), or another update rule;
4. the sign and magnitude of the eigenvalues.

Thus the honest conclusion is:
\[
\text{positive }B-C
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{sector ordering},
\]
not
\[
\text{positive }B-C
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{proved geometric impossibility}.
\]

## 5.3 Relation to the symmetric branch

The analysis is restricted to the physical branch selected in Paper 1. Uniform scalar branch conditions constrain \(\chi_0\), but they do not determine the ordering of \(\chi_1\) and \(\chi_2\). In particular, the tangent condition is a statement about the uniform direction and cannot by itself close the nonuniform sector-ordering problem.

**[EXACT]** This separation of uniform and nonuniform response sectors follows from the \(S_N\) decomposition.

# Section 7: Proof Barriers and Possible Escape Mechanisms

## 7.1 Four completed algebraic attacks

Four nonredundant symbolic investigations have been completed.

### Attack A — Local reduced-density-matrix and correlator identity

The symmetric two-qubit reduced state can be parameterized by collective correlators. Differentiating the entropy gives an exact expression for \(B-C\) in terms of correlator responses and Fréchet derivatives.

**[EXACT]** The reduction identifies the exact terms that would need a sign comparison.

**[EXACT BARRIER]** The resulting response contains entropy-gradient weights and mixed noncommuting terms without a common sign.

### Attack B — Sector ordering from symmetry, local positivity, and tangent data

Permutation symmetry fixes the sector decomposition. Positivity of \(\rho_{12}\), the tangent constraint, and the uniform response bound constrain the symmetric/local data.

**[EXACT BARRIER]** These constraints do not determine an ordering between the nonuniform sector susceptibilities \(\chi_1\) and \(\chi_2\).

### Attack C — Tangent-state susceptibility decomposition

At the tangent point, the response was decomposed into definite and sign-indefinite susceptibility/correlation contributions, and the uniform tangent relation was used to eliminate available variables.

**[EXACT BARRIER]** No sign-controlled inequality was found for the mixed Fréchet/cross-sector terms. The uniform tangent constraint does not dominate them without additional structure.

### Attack D — Schur–Weyl and sector-resolvent analysis

The Hilbert-space and edge-space sectors were related through Schur–Weyl structure and Wigner–Eckart-type matrix elements.

**[EXACT BARRIER]** The two nontrivial edge sectors couple to inequivalent sector-resolvent channels. All-to-all symmetry supplies the decomposition but not an ordering of the corresponding reduced matrix elements or resolvents.

## 7.2 Additional structure that could close the proof

Any degree-budget modification discussed as **Model C** refers to the
degree-budget modification in the project master reference.\footnote{Model C denotes the degree-budget modification discussed in the project master reference.}

A proof of \(B-C>0\) would require at least one of:
1. a sector-resolved resolvent ordering theorem;
2. a positive representation for \(B-C\);
3. a monotonicity theorem for the reduced-state map under edge perturbations;
4. an additional all-to-all identity linking the nonuniform sectors.

These are open mathematical targets, not assumptions silently inserted into the present result.

## 7.3 Possible escape mechanisms

Even if \(B-C>0\) is eventually proved, the linear obstruction could be bypassed by:
- nonlinear mode coupling and saturation;
- a different information measure or non-pairwise feedback rule;
- additional graph constraints or boundary conditions;
- finite-temperature or dynamical-state formulations;
- a different microscopic Hamiltonian.

**[OPEN]** None of these mechanisms has been established in the present paper.

# Section 8: Conclusion

The exact content of this paper is the representation-theoretic reduction of the edge response:
\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{edge}}
=
\mathcal V_0\oplus\mathcal V_1\oplus\mathcal V_2,
\]
together with
\[
\chi_1-\chi_2=(N-2)(B-C).
\]

The sign \(B-C>0\) is supported by validated numerical calculations for \(N=4,6,8,10,20\) and by the documented \(h\)-scan at \(N=8\). The \(N=50\) near-\(x_c\) data are unreliable and are excluded.

The resulting statement is a conditional obstruction: if \(B-C>0\), degree-heterogeneous response dominates degree-preserving response at linear order under the specified feedback interpretation. This is not yet a theorem that finite-dimensional geometry cannot emerge.

The central open problem is to prove or disprove \(B-C>0\) algebraically for the all-to-all TFIM. The completed attacks identify the missing ingredient precisely: a sector-resolved sign or ordering theorem for the noncommuting entropy-response and resolvent terms.
