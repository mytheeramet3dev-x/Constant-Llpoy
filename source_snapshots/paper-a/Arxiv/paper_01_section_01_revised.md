# Section 1 — Introduction (Revised)

The emergence of classical spacetime and geometric locality from underlying quantum information constitutes a central problem in modern theoretical physics. Frameworks such as holographic duality and tensor-network constructions have established deep connections between geometry and quantum entanglement. However, most existing formulations begin with additional geometric structure, such as a prescribed boundary geometry, spatial dimensionality, or lattice connectivity. In an ab initio approach to emergent geometry, these structures should not be assumed at the level of the microscopic interaction rule; instead, they should arise from the relational structure of the quantum state.

In this work, we investigate a framework that is background-free with respect to spatial geometry. We consider an \(N\)-qubit system whose microscopic interaction graph is the complete graph \(K_N\), but whose edge weights are generated self-consistently from quantum correlations. The system is governed by an all-to-all transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian,

\[
H[J]=-
\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\sigma_i^z\sigma_j^z
-h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_i^x,
\]

and the pairwise couplings satisfy

\[
J_{ij}=g\,I_{ij}[\Psi_0(\mathbf J)],
\]

where \(g>0\) is the feedback gain, \(\Psi_0(\mathbf J)\) is the ground state generated by the coupling configuration \(\mathbf J\), and

\[
I_{ij}=S(\rho_i)+S(\rho_j)-S(\rho_{ij})
\]

is the quantum mutual information. Because the Hamiltonian depends on correlations of its own ground state, the self-consistency problem is nonlinear. In particular, it contains a trivial solution at \(J_{ij}=0\), while nontrivial solutions may appear only above a feedback threshold.

The first objective of this paper is therefore to characterize the existence and local bifurcation structure of nontrivial self-consistent solutions. We begin with the permutation-symmetric sector, \(J_{ij}=J\), and introduce the dimensionless coupling

\[
x=J/h.
\]

The scaling form of the Hamiltonian implies that the symmetric mutual information can be written as a dimensionless function \(\mathcal G_N(x)\). The fixed-point equation becomes

\[
f(x;\eta)=\eta\mathcal G_N(x)-x=0,
\qquad \eta=\frac gh.
\]

The principal results of this paper are as follows.

1. **Exact threshold scaling.** Defining

   \[
   \alpha_N=\inf_{x>0}\frac{x}{\mathcal G_N(x)},
   \]

   the critical feedback scale obeys the exact dimensional relation

   \[
   g_c(h)=\alpha_N h,
   \]

   whenever the infimum is attained in the interior of the domain. Here \(\alpha_N\) is a system-size-dependent scaling coefficient determined by the dimensionless mutual-information function; no \(N\)-independent universality is claimed.

2. **Local saddle-node structure.** If the threshold point \((x_c,g_c)\) is an interior nondegenerate point satisfying

   \[
   f(x_c;\eta_c)=0,
   \qquad
   \partial_xf(x_c;\eta_c)=0,
   \qquad
   \partial_x^2f(x_c;\eta_c)\neq0,
   \]

   then the local solution set has the saddle-node normal form

   \[
   x_\pm-x_c
   =\pm\sqrt{
   -\frac{2f_\eta}{f_{xx}}(\eta-\eta_c)
   }
   +O(\eta-\eta_c).
   \]

   Thus two local branches emerge on the supercritical side. The branch structure is a local theorem; a statement about the total number of roots over the entire parameter domain requires an additional global root analysis.

3. **Dimensionless branch stability.** Under the dynamic parameterization

   \[
   g=\gamma g_c(h)=\gamma\alpha_Nh,
   \qquad \gamma>1,
   \]

   the dimensionless fixed-point equation is

   \[
   f_\gamma(x)=\gamma\alpha_N\mathcal G_N(x)-x=0.
   \]

   Consequently, the branch location \(x_*\) and the dimensionless slope

   \[
   f_\gamma'(x_*)
   =\gamma\alpha_N\mathcal G_N'(x_*)-1
   \]

   are independent of the absolute energy scale \(h\). This is an exact consequence of dimensional scaling. The coefficient \(\alpha_N\) remains system-size dependent.

The sign of \(f_\gamma'\) distinguishes the local orientation of the two branches. If the self-consistency iteration is explicitly defined by \(x_{n+1}=\gamma\alpha_N\mathcal G_N(x_n)\), its linear iteration stability requires

\[
   \left|\gamma\alpha_N\mathcal G_N'(x_*)\right|<1,
\]

or equivalently \(|1+f_\gamma'(x_*)|<1\). We therefore distinguish uniform scalar iteration stability from full stability in the space of all edge couplings.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the all-to-all TFIM and the self-consistency formulation. Section 3 states the conditional local saddle-node theorem and derives its normal form. Section 4 develops the dynamic scaling relations and separates the fixed-point derivative from the Picard multiplier. Section 5 discusses the physical branch and the symmetry-breaking response sectors. Section 6 concludes with the established results, numerical status, and remaining limitations.
