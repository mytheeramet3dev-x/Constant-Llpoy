# Lemma 5.1: Asymptotic Positivity of \( B-C \)

**Statement:** For the all-to-all transverse-field Ising model, let \( B = \partial I_{12} / \partial J_{13} \) and \( C = \partial I_{12} / \partial J_{34} \). For any fixed finite \( N \ge 4 \), there exists \( \varepsilon_N > 0 \) such that for \( 0 < h/J < \varepsilon_N \) on the permutation-symmetric branch, \( B - C > 0 \).

## Step 1: Schrieffer–Wolff Expansion in the Parity-Even Sector

The Hamiltonian is \( H = H_z + V_x = -\sum_{i<j} J_{ij} \sigma_i^z \sigma_j^z - h \sum_i \sigma_i^x \).
At \( h=0 \), the ground space is two-fold degenerate (spanned by the fully polarized states \( |\mathbf{0}\rangle \) and \( |\mathbf{1}\rangle \)). 
The TFIM preserves the global bit-flip parity \( P = \prod_i \sigma_i^x \). We restrict to the parity-even sector, where the unperturbed ground state is the unique cat state:
\[
|\mathrm{cat}\rangle = \frac{|\mathbf{0}\rangle + |\mathbf{1}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
\]
This state has an unperturbed energy \( E_0 = -J N(N-1)/2 \) at the uniform point. The gap to the first excited states in the parity-even sector (one-flip superpositions) is \( \Delta = 2J(N-1) \). By Kato's analytic perturbation theory, the ground state \( |\Psi\rangle \) and its projector are analytic functions of \( \varepsilon = h/J \) for \( |\varepsilon| < \varepsilon_N \).

## Step 2: One-Flip Truncation and Two-Flip Corrections

The parity-even one-flip basis states are \( |\phi_k^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (|k\rangle + |\bar{k}\rangle) \), where \( |k\rangle \) is the state with only spin \( k \) flipped from \( |\mathbf{0}\rangle \). 
Using Rayleigh-Schrödinger (Schrieffer-Wolff) perturbation theory, the first-order correction is generated by \( V_x \). The matrix elements are \( \langle \phi_k^+ | (-V_x) | \mathrm{cat} \rangle = -h \). 
The normalized ground state to \( O(\varepsilon^2) \) is:
\[
|\Psi\rangle = a |\mathrm{cat}\rangle + \sum_{k=1}^N b_k |\phi_k^+\rangle + \sum_{k<l} c_{kl} |\phi_{kl}^+\rangle + O(\varepsilon^3)
\]
where:
- \( \Delta_k = 2 \sum_{j \ne k} J_{kj} \)
- \( b_k = \frac{h}{\Delta_k} + O(\varepsilon^3) \)
- \( c_{kl} = O(\varepsilon^2) \) (two-flip corrections)
- \( a = 1 - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^N b_k^2 + O(\varepsilon^4) \)

At the uniform point, \( b_k = h/\Delta \) for all \( k \).

## Step 3: Reduced State Block Decomposition and Schur Complement

We trace out the environment \( E = \{3, \dots, N\} \) to obtain \( \rho_{12} = \mathrm{Tr}_E |\Psi\rangle \langle \Psi| \).
In the parity-adapted basis for sites 1 and 2:
\[
|+\rangle = \frac{00+11}{\sqrt{2}}, \quad |-\rangle = \frac{00-11}{\sqrt{2}}, \quad |\psi_+\rangle = \frac{01+10}{\sqrt{2}}, \quad |\psi_-\rangle = \frac{01-10}{\sqrt{2}}
\]
the density matrix block-diagonalizes into a parity-even block (spanning \( |+\rangle, |\psi_+\rangle \)) and a parity-odd block (spanning \( |-\rangle, |\psi_-\rangle \)).

Evaluating the matrix elements up to \( O(\varepsilon^2) \):
- Large block entries (diagonal): 
  \( \langle \pm | \rho_{12} | \pm \rangle = \frac{1 - (b_1^2 + b_2^2)}{2} \pm O(\varepsilon^2) \)
- Small block entries (diagonal): 
  \( \langle \psi_\pm | \rho_{12} | \psi_\pm \rangle = \frac{1}{2} (b_1 \pm b_2)^2 + O(\varepsilon^4) \)
- Off-diagonal coherence: 
  \( \langle \pm | \rho_{12} | \psi_\pm \rangle = \pm \frac{1}{2} a (b_1 \pm b_2) + O(\varepsilon^3) \)

For each block, the Schur complement of the large entry \( L \approx 1/2 \) yields the small eigenvalue \( \lambda_{\min} \):
\[
\lambda_{\min}^{(\pm)} \approx S - X L^{-1} X = \frac{1}{2}(b_1 \pm b_2)^2 - \frac{\left[ \pm \frac{1}{2} (b_1 \pm b_2) \right]^2}{1/2} = 0
\]
This exact algebraic cancellation demonstrates that the \( O(\varepsilon^2) \) terms vanish identically in the Schur complement. Consequently, the small eigenvalues of \( \rho_{12} \) are strictly bounded by \( O(\varepsilon^4) \) for any \( N \ge 4 \).

## Step 4: Entropy Expansion

Because the trace of the small eigenvalues is \( O(\varepsilon^4) \), the sum of the two large eigenvalues must be \( 1 - O(\varepsilon^4) \). 
Explicitly, the large eigenvalues are \( \lambda_{\pm} = \frac{1}{2} \pm b_1 b_2 + O(\varepsilon^4) \).
The von Neumann entropy of the large block is:
\[
S(L) = - \left(\frac{1}{2} + b_1 b_2\right) \ln\left(\frac{1}{2} + b_1 b_2\right) - \left(\frac{1}{2} - b_1 b_2\right) \ln\left(\frac{1}{2} - b_1 b_2\right) = \ln 2 - 2(b_1 b_2)^2 + O(\varepsilon^4)
\]
Since \( 2(b_1 b_2)^2 = O(\varepsilon^4) \), we get \( S(L) = \ln 2 + O(\varepsilon^4) \).
The small block contributes \( S(\lambda_{\min}) = O(\varepsilon^4 |\ln \varepsilon|) \).
Thus, the joint entropy is:
\[
S(\rho_{12}) = \ln 2 + O(\varepsilon^4 |\ln \varepsilon|)
\]
For the single-site marginals, \( \rho_1 = \frac{1}{2} I_2 + b_1 \sigma_x + O(\varepsilon^2) \), which gives \( S(\rho_1) = \ln 2 - 2b_1^2 + O(\varepsilon^4) \).
The mutual information is therefore:
\[
I_{12} = S(\rho_1) + S(\rho_2) - S(\rho_{12}) = \ln 2 - 2(b_1^2 + b_2^2) + O(\varepsilon^4 |\ln \varepsilon|)
\]

## Step 5: Derivative Control (Remainder Lemma)

By Kato's theorem, the ground state and hence \( \rho_{12} \) are analytic in \( \varepsilon \) and smooth in the edge perturbations \( J_{13}, J_{34} \) as long as the spectral gap remains open. The remainder term \( R_N = O(\varepsilon^4 |\ln \varepsilon|) \) arises from the Fréchet derivative of \( -x \ln x \) near zero. 
Because the small eigenvalues are strictly \( O(\varepsilon^4) \), their parameter derivatives \( \partial_{J_e} \lambda_{\min} \) are bounded by \( O(\varepsilon^4 / J) \). Thus, the derivative of the entropy remainder is bounded by:
\[
|\partial_{J_e} R_N| \le \frac{K_N \varepsilon^4}{J} (1 + |\ln \varepsilon|)
\]
This confirms that differentiation does not destroy the leading asymptotic order.

## Step 6: Edge-Dependent Perturbation and Positivity

We evaluate the derivatives at the uniform point. The gap for site \( k \) is \( \Delta_k^{(z)} = 2 \sum_{j \ne k} J_{kj} \).
For the shared edge \( J_{13} \):
\[
\partial_{J_{13}} \Delta_1^{(z)} = 2 \implies \partial_{J_{13}} b_1 = \partial_{J_{13}} \left(\frac{h}{\Delta_1^{(z)}}\right) = -\frac{2h}{\Delta^2}
\]
\[
\partial_{J_{13}} \Delta_2^{(z)} = 0 \implies \partial_{J_{13}} b_2 = 0
\]
Thus, \( \partial_{J_{13}} (b_1^2 + b_2^2) = 2b_1 \partial_{J_{13}}b_1 = -\frac{4h^2}{\Delta^3} \).

For the disjoint edge \( J_{34} \):
\[
\partial_{J_{34}} \Delta_1^{(z)} = 0, \quad \partial_{J_{34}} \Delta_2^{(z)} = 0 \implies \partial_{J_{34}} (b_1^2 + b_2^2) = 0
\]

Applying these to the mutual information expansion:
\[
B = \partial_{J_{13}} I_{12} = \frac{8h^2}{\Delta^3} + \partial_{J_{13}} R_N
\]
\[
C = \partial_{J_{34}} I_{12} = 0 + \partial_{J_{34}} R_N
\]
Subtracting the two yields:
\[
B - C = \frac{8h^2}{\Delta^3} + O\left( \frac{h^4}{J^5} |\ln(h/J)| \right) = \frac{h^2}{J^3 (N-1)^3} + O\left( \frac{h^4}{J^5} |\ln(h/J)| \right)
\]
Since \( h^2 / J^3 (N-1)^3 > 0 \), there exists an \( \varepsilon_N > 0 \) such that for all \( 0 < h/J < \varepsilon_N \), \( B - C > 0 \). 
This completes the proof of asymptotic positivity.
